Why Professional Storm Damage Repair Matters

Storm damage creates complex, connected repair needs that take experienced restoration professionals to handle properly. Wet insulation, soaked roof decking and damp wall cavities often sit behind a leak that appears small, and without proper detection and drying they bring mold and a weakening structure. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Temporary protection such as tarps and board-up needs correct technique or it will not hold. An improperly installed tarp can create new problems by channeling water or failing under wind load. Experienced in storm mitigation, our team picks appropriate methods and materials to keep the building protected until permanent repair work can be scheduled.

Warning Signs for Storm Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Rooms that start to smell stale or moldy following a storm
  • Shingles that are gone, cracked or curled up when you look from the yard
  • Water in the lowest level after storm drains back up or overflow
  • Attic or wall insulation that is damp or matted down following a storm
  • Rain blown in around door frames, window frames or openings in the wall
  • Sagging or detached gutters that spill rainwater down the siding
  • Ceiling drywall that sags or bulges, or paint that bubbles after rain

Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
  • Learn where the main water shutoff is and how to close it if pipes are damaged in a storm
  • For insurance purposes, keep a yearly set of pictures showing the state of your property
  • Check window and door seals each year and replace any caulking that has cracked
  • Extend downspouts and slope the soil so runoff moves away from the foundation
  • Good attic insulation and ventilation stop ice dams from forming along the roof edge
