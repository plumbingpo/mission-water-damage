What to Do Before Our Austin Crew Arrives

What you do before help reaches your Austin address has a real effect on how the job goes. Safety comes before saving anything. Keep out of water that touches sockets, appliances or wiring, and only cut the power if the breaker box is in a dry place you can get to safely. If the water is from a sewer or toilet backup, do not touch it at all.

Then shut the water off. Use the small valve behind the toilet or under the sink, or go straight to the main. After that, carry light items such as rugs, documents, electronics and chairs somewhere dry. Put foil or plastic under the legs of heavy furniture so the wood does not stain the carpet.

Before mopping, record the damage on your phone, since an insurance company will ask what it looked like at the start. Mop or towel up small amounts of clean water if it is safe, and leave the rest to the crew heading to Austin.

Preventing Water Damage in Austin Homes

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. For homeowners in Austin, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Plumbing comes first. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. The roof and the ground outside are third. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Going away for more than a few days? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. Homeowners in Austin can ask Mission Restoration Co about any of this. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.

Restoration Challenges Austin Homeowners Face

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Austin households. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: An overflowing toilet is the most common bathroom emergency we handle in Austin. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: A long, heavy rain can overwhelm street drains and send water across the yard and into the house. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our Mission Restoration Co crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.
