Kitchen Water Damage in Mission

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in Mission, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Warning Signs

  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry in Place

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
