Crawl Space Water Removal in Mission

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

What It Looks Like

  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Damp air rises into the living space. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Termites and Other Pests

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

Water That Is Not Clean

Water under a house is often not clean. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Within 24 to 48 hours, growth can start on wet joists and the paper facing of batts. A crawl space is dark and still, which gives it little chance to dry on its own. The sooner the water is out and the air is dry, the less mold has to work with. If we find mold on the framing, it gets cleaned and an antimicrobial is applied.
Pumping out the water usually takes a few hours. Drying the framing takes longer, typically 3 to 5 days with a dehumidifier and air movers running under the house. It can run longer when the beams are large, the ground is uncovered, or the flooding went unnoticed for weeks. The job ends when the wood tests dry, whatever the date says.
A damp patch of soil after heavy rain is common, but standing water is not normal and should not be left. Just an inch or two will hold humidity at a level where mold grows and lumber rots. If water is still there a day after the rain stops, or shows up in dry weather, have it checked.
The fix follows from the source of the water. A plumbing leak needs a repair, while rain and groundwater problems usually call for better drainage outside. Clean gutters, downspouts that discharge away from the house and soil graded to slope outward all help. After drying, we tell you what we found and what would keep the space dry.
For a little clean water, a shop vac is fine. It will not dry joists and subfloor, and a box fan mostly moves damp air around. The space is cramped and dark as well, with a chance of live wires or sewage down there. Anything beyond a shallow puddle is better pumped out and dried by a crew with the right gear.
