Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Mission

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

After extracting the water, our Mission crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.
  • Condition of the stand or subfloor below the heater, which may need rebuilding before the replacement is set.
  • How long the tank kept refilling before the supply valve was closed.
  • Quantity of insulation and drywall, on walls or ceilings, that must come out.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Extract the Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.

What Happens if You Wait

Shared Garage Wall

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Platform and Subfloor

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.
